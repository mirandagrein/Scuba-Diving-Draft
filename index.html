Introduction

This project presents a data-driven analysis of scuba-diving fatalities reported by Divers Alert Network between 2010 and 2020. The study examines temporal, geographic, and incident-level patterns in order to identify the factors most consistently associated with fatal outcomes and to evaluate how those patterns can inform prevention.

The analysis integrates descriptive statistics, geographic comparison, and network-based modeling of fatal pathways. Across the dataset, fatalities appear concentrated within specific regions and countries, substantial counts occur in shallow-to-moderate depth ranges, and recurring breathing-related and ascent-related mechanisms emerge across multiple years. Network analysis further demonstrates that varied incident pathways frequently converge toward drowning, while cardiovascular and neurological disabling injuries function as important bridge points in fatal progression.

Taken together, these findings provide an evidence-based framework for understanding where risk is concentrated, how fatal events develop, and which points in the pathway may offer the greatest opportunity for intervention. The sections that follow present the project background, methodology, findings, and conclusions in a structured and accessible format.

Main insight
Fatalities are concentrated rather than evenly spread across geography, depth, and incident categories.

Action: prioritize interventions where concentration is highest instead of spreading resources evenly.

Key limitation
Unknown values remain one of the most important obstacles to interpretation.

Action: improve data collection standards so future analyses can test depth and certification effects more reliably.

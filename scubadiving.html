Understand Scuba Diving

Core diving concepts that help interpret fatality pathways and risk patterns

Why this page matters

This section provides core context for readers who are not divers. The findings in this project use terms such as trigger, mechanism, disabling injury, and cause of death. Understanding basic scuba systems helps explain why those stages connect so strongly in fatality pathways.

Basic dive system

  • Gas supply: A cylinder and regulator deliver breathing gas under pressure.
  • Buoyancy control: Divers use a BCD and weights to control ascent and descent.
  • Environment: Depth, current, visibility, and temperature change workload and stress.
  • Buddy support: Most dives rely on paired monitoring and assistance.

How incidents escalate

Many incidents start with a manageable problem (equipment issue, panic, exertion, ascent error), then rapidly escalate underwater. Limited time, limited breathing reserve, and pressure effects make recovery harder than in surface emergencies.

Simple pathway view: Trigger → Mechanism → Disabling injury → Cause of death.

This flow is the foundation for the network model used in the findings section.

Key terms used in this project

  • Trigger: Initiating event that disrupts a safe dive.
  • Mechanism: Process that converts the trigger into physiological harm.
  • Disabling injury: Immediate incapacitating condition (e.g., respiratory or cardiovascular compromise).
  • Cause of death: Final medical determination of fatal outcome.

Connection to your findings

Your analysis shows that fatalities are not random single events. They frequently follow recurring routes, especially pathways that converge on drowning after respiratory or cardiovascular compromise. This page helps readers interpret that result with practical diving context.

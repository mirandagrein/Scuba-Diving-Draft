Conclusion

Summary of findings and implications for future research

This project addresses the research question by showing that scuba-fatality patterns from 2010–2020 are concentrated across specific regions, countries, depth ranges, triggers, and mechanisms, rather than being evenly distributed. Proportion-based analysis across years reveals both patterns and notable year-to-year shifts.

It also highlights a practical issue for safety research that high frequencies of “Unknown” categories can mask true causal patterns and should be investigated.

This project is incomplete and has yet to broaden the data analysis into deeper insights, so the research question has not fully been answered yet. While the project does not establish incident-level causality, it provides evidence on distributional patterns and potential priority areas for inquiry which will be crucial in developing this project further to the final stages.

Limitations

Results depend on the consistency and completeness of the data which had "Unknown" values. Tables limit the ability to model individual-level interactions which is the strongest way to perform analysis. Missing values also reduce the effectiveness of interpretation.

Future Questions

This project raises several next-step questions: How would patterns change if “Unknown” were reduced through improved reporting? Which country- or region-specific contexts explain shifts in annual composition? Do certain trigger-mechanism combinations become more prominent in particular depth ranges or diver-experience groups?
